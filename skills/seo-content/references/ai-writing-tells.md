# AI writing tells

Patterns readers and detectors associate with machine-written text. Scan drafts for them before publishing.

## Punctuation

- **Em dashes** are the strongest single tell. Use commas, colons, parentheses or a new sentence. More than one em dash on a page is a signal to revise.
- Semicolons in casual prose; colons used as dramatic pauses; excessive bolding.

## Overused verbs

| Avoid | Use |
|-------|-----|
| delve into | look at, examine |
| leverage | use |
| utilise | use |
| facilitate | help, enable |
| foster | encourage, build |
| bolster | strengthen |
| underscore | stress, highlight |
| unveil | show, introduce |
| navigate (a landscape) | handle, manage |
| streamline | simplify |
| enhance | improve |
| elucidate | explain |
| ascertain | find out |
| endeavour | try |

## Overused adjectives

robust, comprehensive, pivotal, crucial, vital, transformative, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, innovative, seamless, intricate, nuanced, multifaceted, holistic, game-changing, powerful, dynamic.

Use: strong, complete, key, important, new, smooth, complex, detailed, or a specific fact instead of an adjective.

## Transitions and openers

Avoid: furthermore, moreover, notwithstanding, that being said, at its core, to put it simply, it is worth noting that, in the realm of, in the landscape of, in today's fast-paced world, in today's digital age, in an era of, in the ever-evolving, imagine a world where, let's delve into, this begs the question, in conclusion, to sum up, at the end of the day, all things considered, in the final analysis.

Use: also, and, but, however, so, for example, first, next, finally, in short.

## Structural patterns

- "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]..."
- "It's not just [X], it's [Y]."
- "Think of [X] as [elaborate metaphor]."
- Sentences starting "By [verb]-ing, you can..."
- Rule-of-three lists in every paragraph.
- Every paragraph the same length; every sentence the same length.
- A summary sentence restating the paragraph at the end of the paragraph.
- Headings that are questions followed by "Great question".
- Closing with "Happy [verb]-ing!" or an upbeat call to action.

## Filler and empty intensifiers

absolutely, actually, basically, certainly, clearly, definitely, essentially, extremely, fundamentally, incredibly, interestingly, naturally, obviously, quite, really, significantly, simply, surely, truly, ultimately, undoubtedly, very.

Delete, or replace with a specific fact.

## Academic padding

| Avoid | Use |
|-------|-----|
| shed light on | explain |
| pave the way for | enable |
| a myriad of / a plethora of | many |
| paramount | essential |
| pertaining to | about |
| prior to / subsequent to | before / after |
| in light of | because of |
| with respect to / in terms of | about, for |
| the fact that | that |

## Self-check

1. Read it aloud; rewrite anything you would not say to a colleague.
2. Count em dashes; count the words above.
3. Check sentence length variety and paragraph length variety.
4. Make sure every intensifier earns its place.
5. Replace generic examples with ones from the project's domain.
6. Add one concrete detail per section that only someone who did the work would know.
