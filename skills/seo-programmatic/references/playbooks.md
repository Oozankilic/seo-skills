# Programmatic SEO playbooks

Notes per playbook: what makes the page valuable, where the data comes from, what to avoid.

## Templates ("[type] template")
- Value: the template itself, previewable and downloadable; use-case notes; variations.
- Data: your own template library; usage data (most popular, ratings).
- Avoid: one generic template renamed per type.

## Curation ("best [category]")
- Value: real selection criteria, hands-on notes, pros and cons, pricing, who each option fits.
- Data: your own testing, user ratings, public specs.
- Avoid: affiliate-style lists with no criteria; copying competitor lists.

## Conversions ("[X] to [Y]")
- Value: a working converter, formula, worked examples, related conversions.
- Data: units, currencies, rates (with update time shown).
- Avoid: pages for pairs nobody searches.

## Comparisons ("[X] vs [Y]")
- Value: a decision for a specific reader: table of differences, pricing, best for, migration notes; honest about where you lose.
- Data: feature matrices you maintain, pricing pages (dated), public reviews.
- Avoid: identical table with vendor names swapped; one-sided marketing.

## Alternatives ("[competitor] alternatives")
- Value: a list of real alternatives including yours, framed around the reasons people leave the competitor (price, missing feature, support).
- Data: competitor pricing and feature changes, review-site complaints, your own switching data.
- Avoid: naming only yourself.

## Examples ("[type] examples")
- Value: curated, annotated examples with what to copy from each; screenshots.
- Data: your own gallery, customer work with permission.
- Avoid: unannotated galleries.

## Locations ("[service] in [location]")
- Value: local specifics: providers, prices, regulations, neighbourhoods, hours, local reviews, a map, a person to contact.
- Data: your locations or providers, local data sources, first-party reviews.
- Avoid: same copy per city; locations you do not serve; fake addresses.

## Personas ("[product] for [audience]")
- Value: the audience's workflow, their specific problems, features that matter to them, customer stories from that segment, integrations they use.
- Data: segment usage data, interviews, case studies.
- Avoid: one generic product page with the audience name swapped.

## Integrations ("[A] [B] integration")
- Value: what the integration does, setup steps, use cases, screenshots, limits, data mapping; a working connector.
- Data: your integration catalog and docs.
- Avoid: pages for integrations that do not exist ("coming soon" farms).

## Glossary ("what is [term]")
- Value: a clear definition in the first sentence, context, examples, related terms, a link to the deeper guide; good for featured snippets and AI citation.
- Data: your domain expertise; internal links to pillar content.
- Avoid: one-paragraph stubs with no links; duplicating a competitor's glossary.

## Translations
- Value: proper localisation (currency, examples, regulations), not machine translation alone; translated slugs, titles, descriptions.
- Data: your source pages plus translators or reviewed machine translation.
- Avoid: canonicalising translations to the source language; missing hreflang.

## Directory ("[category] tools")
- Value: a structured, filterable catalog with your own data fields (pricing, ratings, last update), category hubs, individual listing pages.
- Data: proprietary or user-submitted listings, verified.
- Avoid: scraped listings with no added fields; empty categories.

## Profiles ("[entity name]")
- Value: facts about an entity (company, person, product, place) with sourced data, history, related entities.
- Data: proprietary datasets, public records with attribution.
- Avoid: pages on entities with no demand; personal data issues.

## Layering

Combine playbooks when demand supports it: "best [category] in [city]", "[product] for [audience] vs [competitor]", "[A] [B] integration examples". Keep the hub structure consistent so every layer has a parent page.
