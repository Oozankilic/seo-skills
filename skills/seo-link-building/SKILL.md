# Link building and mentions

Links and mentions from relevant, editorial pages raise authority and, increasingly, decide whether AI engines name the brand. The work is: have something worth referencing, find the pages that reference things like it, ask well, and keep records.

Read `seo-context.md` for positioning (why anyone would cite this site) and competitors (whose backlink profiles to mine).

## 1. The linkable asset

Pick or create something a page would reference: original data or a study, a free tool or calculator, a template library, a definitive guide, a comparison with real numbers, a strong opinion, an open-source project, a dataset. A product page is rarely linkable; a "state of X" report is.

## 2. Prospecting

Build five to ten queries from the asset or topic, then search them (a SERP tool, or manual searches) and keep the pages that already link to things like yours:

- `<topic> resources`
- `best <category> tools`
- `<competitor> alternatives`
- `<topic> statistics`
- `<topic> guide`
- `<topic> examples`
- `<topic> templates`
- `<topic> software`
- `<topic> for <audience>`
- `"<competitor>" -site:competitor.com` (pages mentioning a competitor)

Mine competitor backlinks when a backlink tool is available (OpenSEO backlink overview, or any index): pages linking to two or more competitors but not to you are the best prospects. For AI visibility, the sources cited in AI answers (see `seo-ai-visibility`) are the prospect list.

Keep: editorial articles, listicles, resource pages, comparisons, statistics round-ups, templates galleries, directories with editorial review, community threads that stay active.
Drop: homepages, login pages, thin affiliate pages, spam, unrelated forums, direct competitors (unless a comparison angle is valid), obvious paid placements (flag them).

## 3. Directory submissions

A repeatable baseline for new products: product and startup directories, review sites, "tools" catalogs, app marketplaces, community launch sites, industry associations, local chambers. Build a list of the directories relevant to the category (there are a couple of hundred general ones; the relevant subset is usually 30 to 80) and submit in batches. Tools such as SoPilot automate form filling for this; a spreadsheet and an afternoon work too.

Rules: consistent name, one-line description and logo; correct category; the canonical URL (no tracking parameters unless you want them); skip directories that require reciprocal links or payment for a follow link unless the listing has real referral value; record where you submitted and when.

## 4. The angle, per prospect

- **Missing or broken resource**: their page links to something dead or outdated; you have the replacement.
- **Better current data**: your study or numbers update their claim.
- **Useful tool or template**: fits their list.
- **Inclusion in a list or comparison**: you belong on their "best X" or "X alternatives" page; say why in one line, with proof.
- **Expert quote or source**: offer a specific quote or data point for an article they are updating.
- **Mention correction**: they named you without a link, or linked to the wrong page.

## 5. Contact discovery

Look for author bylines and author pages, contact and editorial-guidelines pages, about and team pages, LinkedIn or X profiles, newsletter mastheads, public email addresses in the page or in `Person` / `Organization` structured data. Record only what you found, with the source URL. Never invent names, emails or handles. If nothing turns up, say which path to try next.

## 6. Outreach drafts

Short, specific, honest. One ask. Name the exact page and the exact spot. Lead with what is in it for their readers.

**List inclusion**

> Subject: [Tool] for your "[list title]" page
>
> Hi [Name], your list of [category] at [URL] is the one I send people to. One gap: [Tool], which [one-line differentiator with a number]. If it fits, here is the page: [URL]. Happy to send a screenshot or a short blurb in your format.

**Update or data**

> Subject: Updated [statistic] for your [topic] article
>
> Hi [Name], in [article] you cite [old figure] from [year]. We published [new figure] in [study, year, sample size]: [URL]. If useful for your next update, the chart is reusable with attribution.

**Broken link**

> Subject: Dead link on [page]
>
> Hi [Name], the link to [resource] on [URL] returns a 404. If you want a replacement, [your page] covers the same ground and is current as of [date].

Personalise each: a sentence showing you read the page. No mass sends, no templated flattery, no follow-up more than once or twice.

## 7. Backlink hygiene

- Check the profile when a tool is available: lost links (reclaim by fixing redirects or asking), links to 404s on your site (redirect), unlinked brand mentions (ask for a link), anchor text diversity.
- Disavow only with clear evidence of a manipulative link scheme pointed at the site; Google ignores most spam on its own.
- Mark paid or sponsored links on your own site with `rel="sponsored"`; user-generated with `rel="ugc"`.

## Output

1. **The angle**: best outreach angle and prospect type to work first.
2. **Prospects**: URL | site | how found | angle | contact path (with source) | priority.
3. **Why these**: evidence per prospect worth explaining.
4. **Outreach drafts**: two or three reusable angles, personalised per top prospect.
5. **Directory batch**: list with category, URL, status.
6. **Limitations**: contacts not found, likely paid placements, competitors.
7. **What to do next**: send order and timing.

## Guardrails

- Never buy links, join link schemes or exchange links at scale; never spin guest posts.
- Never invent contact details; attribute every contact to its source.
- Flag prospects that are competitors or paid placements.
- Personalise; do not mass-mail.
- Outreach sends on the user's behalf need the user's explicit go-ahead; draft, do not send.
